overview:
  - Eco-Nexus House redefines cold climate sustainability through a 4-plex that marries Ithaca's historical resilience with cutting-edge green innovation. Rooted in the region's timber building legacy, the design harvests "waste as wealth" -- repurposing discarded lumber from local forests and sun-dried aquatic biomass -- into modular, hollow-core bricks. These bricks, layered with hempcrete and cellulose insulation, form a super-insulated shell that locks in warmth while breathing with the seasons. It is not just a home, but also a living bridge between Ithaca's past and its zero-carbon future.

  - type: text

    title: Passive House

    content: It began by generating various architectural massing options and selecting the design that maximized solar exposure. For materials, utilizing recycled bricks created by crushing and heat-molding dried algae from the Ithaca lake region and waste wood from local forests into hollow blocks, thereby achieving both thermal insulation and environmental sustainability. Regarding the wall system, a Trombe wall design was employed to effectively manage solar heat gain during the day and provide thermal protection against the cold at night.
